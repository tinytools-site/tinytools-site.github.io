How GPA works on the 4.0 scale

Grade Point Average condenses your grades into a single number between 0.0 and 4.0. Each letter grade maps to grade points — typically A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0, with plus/minus variants in between (B+ = 3.3, B- = 2.7). Your GPA is the credit-weighted average: multiply each course's points by its credit hours, add them up, and divide by total credits. A 4-credit A counts twice as much as a 2-credit A.

Credit weighting is why GPA math surprises people. An A in a 1-credit lab barely moves the needle, while a C in a 4-credit core course drags hard. If your GPA is below target, the fastest fix is protecting grades in high-credit courses — and, counterintuitively, a B in a 4-credit class helps more than an A in a 1-credit one when you are trying to climb.

Most US colleges use the unweighted 4.0 scale above, where an A is 4.0 no matter how hard the course was. Many high schools use a weighted scale that adds 0.5 or 1.0 points for honors and AP classes, which is why you see GPAs above 4.0 on transcripts. This calculator uses the standard unweighted 4.0 scale — if your school weights advanced courses, add the bonus points to those grades first.

For cumulative GPA across semesters, the same formula applies to all courses ever taken: total grade points divided by total credits attempted. Retaken courses follow your school's policy — some replace the old grade, others average both attempts. When in doubt, ask your registrar which policy applies before planning around a retake.

Frequently asked questions

How is GPA calculated?

Multiply each grade's point value by the course's credit hours, sum those products, and divide by total credit hours.

What is a good GPA?

Generally 3.0+ is solid, 3.5+ is strong for graduate school and competitive jobs. Context and major matter too.

What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Unweighted caps at 4.0. Weighted adds bonus points (often +0.5/+1.0) for honors/AP courses, so it can exceed 4.0.

Do plus and minus grades affect GPA?

At most schools, yes: e.g. B+ = 3.3, B = 3.0, B- = 2.7. Some schools use flat letter grades without them.
