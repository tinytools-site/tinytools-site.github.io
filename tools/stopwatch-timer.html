Timing, done right

A stopwatch measures elapsed time upward from zero — for workouts, experiments, cooking steps or anything you want to time. A countdown timer does the reverse: you set a duration and it alerts you at zero. This page combines both, because most timing tasks need one or the other and switching tools mid-task is annoying.

Browser timers are accurate to a few milliseconds for everyday purposes. The implementation here does not count ticks — it records timestamps and computes the difference, so brief hiccups do not accumulate into drift. One honest caveat: browsers throttle timers in background tabs to save battery, so for critical timing keep the tab visible or accept roughly second-level accuracy.

Lap and split times turn a stopwatch into a training tool. Runners log each kilometre split to pace races; cooks time each stage of a recipe; presenters rehearse sections against the clock. The lap list below records every split with its own duration and the running total, and you can copy the table into a training log.

The countdown ends with a sound generated in your browser — no audio files needed. Set minutes and seconds, start it, and a chime plays at zero even if the tab is in the background (audio usually still fires; the display just updates when you return). For focus sessions with structured work/break intervals, the Pomodoro timer on this site is the better fit.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the online stopwatch?

Within a few milliseconds for normal use. It uses timestamps rather than counting ticks, so it doesn't drift.

Does the timer work in a background tab?

Mostly — the countdown completes and the sound plays, but the display may update late because browsers throttle hidden tabs.

Can I record lap times?

Yes. Press Lap while the stopwatch runs; each split shows its own duration and the running total.

Is there a sound when the countdown ends?

Yes — a chime generated in your browser. Make sure your device volume is on and the tab is allowed to play sound.
