How to use the Percentage Calculator

Percentages show up everywhere: shop discounts, restaurant tips, tax rates, salary raises and exam scores. A percentage is simply a number expressed as a fraction of 100, so 25% means 25 out of every 100. This calculator handles the three calculations people need most, without you having to remember the formulas.

To find X percent of Y, multiply Y by X and divide by 100. For example, a 20% discount on an $80 item is 80 × 20 ÷ 100 = $16 off, so you pay $64. To find what percentage X is of Y, divide X by Y and multiply by 100 — a score of 42 out of 60 is 42 ÷ 60 × 100 = 70%. For percentage change, subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. A price rising from $50 to $65 is a 30% increase.

One common source of confusion is the difference between a percentage change and percentage points. If an interest rate moves from 4% to 6%, that is a 2 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase. News headlines sometimes mix these up, so it pays to check which one is meant.

The reverse-percentage mode answers questions like: if $45 is the price after a 10% discount, what was the original price? The answer is $50. This is handy when you only know the final amount and the rate that was applied. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate X percent of Y?

Multiply Y by X, then divide by 100. For example, 15% of 200 is 200 × 15 ÷ 100 = 30. You can also use the first mode of the calculator above.

How do I find the percentage increase between two numbers?

Subtract the original number from the new number, divide the result by the original number, and multiply by 100. Use the percentage-change mode above and it does this automatically.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A move from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage point increase, but a 50% relative increase. Percentage points measure the absolute difference between two rates.

How do I reverse a percentage, e.g. find the original price?

If you know the final value and the percentage applied, divide the final value by (1 ± rate). The calculator's reverse mode handles discounts and markups for you.
