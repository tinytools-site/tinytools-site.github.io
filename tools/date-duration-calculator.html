Calculating the time between two dates

Counting the days between two dates sounds simple until you try it by hand across month and year boundaries. The reliable method is to convert both dates to a day count (days since a fixed reference) and subtract — which is exactly what this calculator does using your browser's date handling, including leap years. February 29ths are counted only when they actually fall inside the range.

The breakdown into years, months and days uses calendar arithmetic rather than fixed conversions, because months are not all the same length. A span from March 15 to June 15 is exactly 3 months, while March 15 to June 14 is 2 months and 30 days. This matches how people naturally describe durations (“three months”) and how contracts usually measure them.

The business-day count excludes Saturdays and Sundays, which is what project plans, shipping estimates and service-level agreements usually mean by “days”. Public holidays are not excluded because they vary by country and region — if your count needs to skip holidays, subtract them from the business-day total manually.

Common uses include: days until a deadline or event, exact relationship and employment anniversaries, pregnancy and project timelines, warranty and subscription periods, and historical research (“how many days between these two events?”). The result can include or exclude the end date; this calculator counts the difference exclusively, so January 1 to January 2 is 1 day.

Frequently asked questions

How many days are between two dates?

Enter both dates above — the calculator counts every calendar day in between, handling leap years automatically.

What is the difference between calendar days and business days?

Calendar days include weekends; business days exclude Saturdays and Sundays. Enable the business-day option for work planning.

Are leap years handled?

Yes. February 29th is counted whenever it falls within your date range.

Does the count include the end date?

No — the difference is exclusive, so Jan 1 to Jan 2 counts as 1 day, matching the standard convention.
