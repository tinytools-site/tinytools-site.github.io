How Much to Tip: A Practical Guide

The standard rates, the awkward cases, and how to split the bill without drama.

Tipping norms keep creeping upward, but the widely accepted baselines are stable. Here is what most etiquette guides still recommend in 2026.

Standard tipping rates

  • Sit-down restaurant: 15–20% of the pre-tax bill (20% is now the default for good service).
  • Food delivery: 15–20%, with a $3–$5 minimum even on small orders.
  • Coffee shop / counter: 10% or $1–$2; optional for simple drip coffee.
  • Bar: $1–$2 per drink, or 15–20% of the tab.
  • Taxi / rideshare: 10–15%.
  • Hotel housekeeping: $2–$5 per night, left daily.

Pre-tax or post-tax?

Traditional etiquette says tip on the pre-tax subtotal, since tax is not service. On a $100 bill with 8% tax, 20% pre-tax is $20.00 versus $21.60 post-tax — the difference is small, so do not stress about it.

Splitting the bill fairly

The clean way: divide the total (including tax and tip) equally only if everyone ordered similarly. Otherwise each person covers their items plus a proportional share of tax and tip. Rounding up to the nearest dollar keeps the math painless.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good tip percentage at a restaurant?

15-20% of the pre-tax bill is standard; 20% is the common default for good service.

Should you tip on takeout?

It's optional but appreciated &mdash; 10% is a generous gesture for takeout orders.

How do you split a restaurant bill fairly?

Each person pays for their items plus a proportional share of tax and tip; split evenly only when orders were similar.
