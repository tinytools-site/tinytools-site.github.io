What Is a Good BMI by Age and Gender?

The standard BMI categories, how age and gender matter, and what BMI misses.

Body Mass Index (BMI) = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)2. It is a screening tool, not a diagnosis — but it is the standard starting point doctors use.

Standard adult BMI categories (WHO)

  • Below 18.5 — Underweight
  • 18.5–24.9 — Healthy range
  • 25–29.9 — Overweight
  • 30 and above — Obese

Does age change what is healthy?

The WHO categories are the same for all adults, but research shows slightly higher BMIs can be fine — even protective — in older adults. For people over 65, many geriatricians consider 23–30 acceptable, because a little extra reserve helps during illness. For children and teens, BMI is interpreted with age-and-gender percentiles, not the adult cutoffs.

Does gender matter?

The formula is identical for men and women, but body composition differs: at the same BMI, women typically carry more body fat than men. That is why waist circumference is often checked alongside BMI — under 94 cm (37 in) for men and under 80 cm (31.5 in) for women are common targets.

What BMI does not tell you

BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat. Athletes, very muscular people, and pregnant women get misleading results. Treat it as one data point among several: waist size, activity level, blood pressure and blood work complete the picture.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI for adults?

18.5 to 24.9 is the WHO healthy range for adults of all ages.

Is BMI accurate for muscular people?

No &mdash; muscle weighs more than fat, so muscular people can show an 'overweight' BMI while being lean. Body-fat percentage is a better metric for them.

Does healthy BMI change with age?

Official cutoffs stay the same, but many doctors accept a slightly higher range (up to ~30) for adults over 65.
