How to Calculate Monthly Loan Payments

The amortization formula, explained simply — with real numbers.

Every fixed-rate loan — mortgage, car loan, personal loan — uses the same amortization formula to turn a lump sum into equal monthly payments:

M = P × r(1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)

  • M = monthly payment
  • P = loan principal (amount borrowed)
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12)
  • n = total number of payments (years × 12)

Worked example: $300,000 mortgage at 6% for 30 years

P = 300,000, r = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005, n = 360. Plug in: M = 300,000 × 0.005 × (1.005)360 ÷ ((1.005)360 − 1) = $1,798.65/month. Over 30 years you pay about $347,500 in interest — more than the loan itself.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Each payment first covers that month's interest, and only the remainder reduces principal. Early on, the balance is large so interest eats most of the payment; later, the split flips. Extra principal payments early in the loan save disproportionately more interest.

What the formula does not include

Real mortgage quotes add property tax, homeowner's insurance and PMI. The formula gives principal + interest only — always ask lenders for the full PITI figure.

Frequently asked questions

How is the monthly payment on a loan calculated?

With the amortization formula M = P*r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where P is principal, r is the monthly rate, and n is the number of payments.

Does a longer loan term always mean lower payments?

Yes, monthly payments drop &mdash; but total interest rises sharply. A 30-year mortgage can cost double the interest of a 15-year one.

What is amortization?

The process of paying off a loan through fixed installments, where each payment is split between interest and principal.
