How to Calculate Age Exactly

Why ‘subtract the years’ is wrong half the time — and the right method.

Age looks simple — current year minus birth year — but that naive subtraction is wrong until your birthday has passed this year.

The correct method

  1. Subtract the years: current year − birth year.
  2. If this year's birthday has not happened yet, subtract 1.
  3. For months and days, count forward from your last birthday to today.

Example: born 14 March 2008, today is 6 October 2026. 2026 − 2008 = 18, and the March birthday already passed, so the age is 18 years, 6 months and 22 days.

How leap years are handled

People born on 29 February legally age on 28 February (or 1 March, depending on jurisdiction) in non-leap years. Age calculators handle this by treating Feb 29 birthdays as Feb 28 in common years.

Where exact age matters

  • School and sports enrollment with strict cutoff dates.
  • Retirement and benefits eligibility calculated to the day.
  • Medical dosing for children, often based on age in months.

Frequently asked questions

How is age calculated from date of birth?

Subtract the birth year from the current year, then subtract one more year if this year's birthday hasn't happened yet.

How do you calculate age in months?

Count full months since birth: (current year - birth year) x 12 + (current month - birth month), adjusting if the day hasn't been reached.

What happens to Feb 29 birthdays in non-leap years?

Most systems treat them as Feb 28 (some as March 1) for legal and administrative purposes.
