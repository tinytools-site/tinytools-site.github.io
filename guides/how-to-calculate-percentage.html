How to Calculate Percentage (3 Formulas You Need)

The three percentage formulas everyone uses — with dead-simple examples.

Almost every percentage problem is one of three types. Master these and you will never be confused again.

1. What is X% of Y?

(X ÷ 100) × Y. Example: 20% of 250 = 0.20 × 250 = 50.

2. What percent is X of Y?

(X ÷ Y) × 100. Example: 45 is what percent of 180? 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%.

3. Percentage increase or decrease

((new − old) ÷ old) × 100. Example: a price rises from $80 to $100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase. Note: a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not return to the original — 100 × 1.5 × 0.5 = 75.

Common real-world uses

  • Discounts: 30% off $120 means you pay 70% — 0.70 × 120 = $84.
  • Tips: 20% of a $64.50 bill = $12.90.
  • Grades: 38 correct out of 45 questions = 38 ÷ 45 × 100 = 84.4%.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 15% of a number?

Multiply the number by 0.15. For example, 15% of 200 = 0.15 x 200 = 30.

How do I find the percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value, and multiply by 100.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

A rise from 10% to 15% is a 5 percentage-point increase, but a 50% relative increase. News headlines often blur the two.
