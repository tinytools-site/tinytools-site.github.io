How to Calculate GPA (Weighted and Unweighted)

Your GPA in plain English: the formulas, the 4.0 scale, and worked examples.

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is the average of your course grades, converted to a standard 4.0 scale. Colleges and employers use it as a quick summary of academic performance. There are two common versions: unweighted (every course counts equally, max 4.0) and weighted (harder courses like honors or AP get bonus points, often up to 5.0).

The unweighted GPA formula

Convert each letter grade to points: A = 4.0, B = 3.0, C = 2.0, D = 1.0, F = 0 (plus/minus variants like B+ = 3.3 and B- = 2.7 on many scales). Then:

GPA = total grade points ÷ number of courses

Example: grades of A, B+, B, C+ become 4.0 + 3.3 + 3.0 + 2.3 = 12.6 points ÷ 4 courses = 3.15 GPA.

How weighted GPA works

Schools add a bonus for advanced courses — commonly +0.5 for honors and +1.0 for AP/IB. So an A in an AP class counts as 5.0 instead of 4.0. The formula is the same, but you use the boosted point values. Weighted GPAs above 4.0 are normal and expected by admissions offices.

Which one do colleges look at?

Most colleges recalculate your GPA on their own scale anyway, but they consider both your GPA and your course rigor. A 3.7 with several AP classes usually beats a 4.0 of easy electives.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good GPA in high school?

A 3.0 is solid, 3.5+ is strong, and 3.7+ is competitive for selective colleges. Context matters: course difficulty and trends count too.

Is a 4.0 GPA weighted or unweighted?

A 4.0 is the maximum on the unweighted scale. Weighted scales go up to 5.0, so a 4.3 GPA is always a weighted figure.

How do I convert percentage grades to GPA?

A common mapping is 90-100 = 4.0, 80-89 = 3.0, 70-79 = 2.0, 60-69 = 1.0, below 60 = 0, though scales vary by school.
