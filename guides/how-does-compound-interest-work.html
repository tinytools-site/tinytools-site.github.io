How Does Compound Interest Work?

Why Einstein supposedly called it the most powerful force in finance — with numbers.

Compound interest means earning interest on your interest. Each period, gains are added to the balance, so the next period's growth is calculated on a bigger number. Over years, this snowball effect dwarfs simple interest.

The formula

A = P(1 + r/n)nt — A is the final amount, P the principal, r the annual rate, n the compounding frequency per year, t the years.

Real example: $10,000 at 7%

  • After 10 years: $19,672
  • After 20 years: $38,697
  • After 30 years: $76,123

Notice the second decade adds far more than the first — that acceleration is compounding at work.

Monthly contributions supercharge it

Adding $200/month to that same $10,000 at 7% for 30 years grows to roughly $325,000 — of which only $82,000 was your own money. Time and consistency beat timing the market.

Compounding frequency matters — a little

Daily compounding beats monthly, which beats annual, but the difference is small compared to the rate and time horizon. A 1% higher return over 30 years matters far more than daily vs. monthly compounding.

Frequently asked questions

What is compound interest in simple terms?

Interest calculated on both your original money and the interest it already earned, so growth accelerates over time.

How powerful is compound interest over 30 years?

At 7% annual return, money roughly multiplies 7.6x in 30 years &mdash; $10,000 becomes about $76,000.

Does compounding frequency matter much?

Slightly. Daily beats monthly beats yearly, but the interest rate and time horizon matter far more.
